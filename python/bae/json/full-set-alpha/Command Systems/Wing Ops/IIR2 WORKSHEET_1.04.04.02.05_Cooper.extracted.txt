1. Leadership Summary & Confidence Ratings
Top Three Messages for Leadership
If leadership only remembers three things from this assessment, what should they be?
1. The contractor has built a theoretically sound planning structure and workflow, but the WBS definition and supporting documents currently do not contain sufficient clarity and information to understand the scope of Control Accounts.
2. The proposed Control Accounts are broad and span across multiple Integrated Product Teams (IPTs). Wing Operations Tactical Admin Network (TAN) products are broken down across a variety of Control Accounts. This structure complicates standard progress tracking, Earned Value Management (EVM), and the ability to track integration between products and workflow (example SW vs HW integration).
3. Conflicting Documentation and Artifacts: Documents continue to change throughout this review process (Documents note that they will continue to be updated until mid to late Sep 2026). New versions of the RAM and WBS dictionary continue to be uploaded throughout the week and have contradictions between different versions. This churn makes review difficult and potentially a waste of effort if the documents are different a month from now.

The Overall Confidence Rating represents the Government's professional judgment of the contractor's current planning maturity and readiness based on the evidence reviewed during IIR #2. It reflects the Government's point-in-time assessment of the contractor's planning architecture and its readiness to continue progressing toward Integrated Master Schedule (IMS) loading, Performance Measurement Baseline (PMB) implementation, and subsequent IIR activities.
Importantly, this rating is not a determination that the IMS or PMB is fully mature, executable, or ready for approval. Instead, it reflects the Government's current confidence that the contractor has established a sufficiently mature planning foundation to support continued planning, integration, and progressive maturation toward Milestone B.

3. Strengths Observed
Document the most significant strengths identified during the assessment that increase confidence in the contractor's planning maturity and readiness. Strengths should be supported by objective evidence and highlight effective planning practices, planning artifacts, integration activities, or management disciplines that demonstrate a well-developed planning architecture and increase confidence in the contractor's planning maturity and readiness to support future Integrated Master Schedule (IMS) development, Performance Measurement Baseline (PMB) implementation, and program execution.
4. Concerns and Risks Observed
Document the most significant concerns identified during the assessment and any associated risks that could reduce confidence in the contractor's planning maturity and readiness or adversely affect future planning, integration, or program execution if not addressed.
Concerns should be supported by objective evidence and identify observed weaknesses, gaps, inconsistencies, or uncertainties that warrant additional management attention. Risks should describe the potential consequences if those concerns remain unresolved. Not every concern creates a program risk, and not every identified risk is currently affecting program execution. The purpose of this section is to identify issues that warrant Government visibility, follow-up, or corrective action as planning continues to mature.
Definitions
Concern: An observed weakness, gap, inconsistency, or uncertainty that may reduce confidence in the contractor's planning maturity and readiness.
Risk: A potential future consequence that could adversely affect planning, cost, schedule, technical performance, integration, PMB implementation, or program execution if a concern is not addressed.
5. Open Questions and Follow-Up Items
Document questions that remain unanswered during the assessment and identify the additional evidence, clarification, documentation, or demonstrations needed from the contractor to complete the evaluation or increase confidence in the assessment findings.
Open Questions and Follow-Up Items identify areas where the available evidence was incomplete, unclear, or inconsistent. These items represent information gaps that should be resolved through additional discussions, demonstrations, or artifact reviews before the Government can fully assess the contractor's planning maturity and readiness. They are not, by themselves, concerns, deficiencies, or risks.
6. Cross-Product/Cross-Function Integration Observations
Document observations regarding the extent to which the assessed product element is integrated with other product elements and Government assessment areas, including the identification, coordination, and management of technical, planning, schedule, and deliverable interfaces necessary to support planning maturity and program execution.
Cross-Product/Cross-Function Integration Observations should summarize how effectively the contractor has identified, coordinated, and incorporated cross-product/cross-function interfaces, dependencies, and integration activities into its planning architecture. Observations should be supported by objective evidence and reflect the maturity of cross-functional planning, the visibility of interdependencies, and the Government's confidence that the assigned product element can be successfully integrated into the overall program.

4. Concerns and Risks Observed
Document the most significant concerns identified during the assessment and any associated risks that could reduce confidence in the contractor's planning maturity and readiness or adversely affect future planning, integration, or program execution if not addressed.
Concerns should be supported by objective evidence and identify observed weaknesses, gaps, inconsistencies, or uncertainties that warrant additional management attention. Risks should describe the potential consequences if those concerns remain unresolved.
Not every concern creates a program risk, and not every identified risk is currently affecting program execution. The purpose of this section is to identify issues that warrant Government visibility, follow-up, or corrective action as planning continues to mature.
Definitions
Concern: An observed weakness, gap, inconsistency, or uncertainty that may reduce confidence in the contractor's planning maturity and readiness.
Risk: A potential future consequence that could adversely affect planning, cost, schedule, technical performance, integration, PMB implementation, or program execution if a concern is not addressed.
Concern 1 (S-4: Organizational Ownership): There is no stable ownership for this control account, with responsibilities constantly shifting between CAMs and IPTs. Risk: This churn guarantees that scope will be dropped, deliverables will be lost, and accountability will be impossible to enforce, severely jeopardizing the success of integration and test activities.
Concern 2 (General): The review was characterized by a lack of configuration control over planning artifacts (RAM, WBS), with multiple conflicting versions in circulation. Risk: This
demonstrates
a systemic failure in process discipline. It forces the Government to make assessments on unstable, unreliable data and prevents the establishment of a firm performance measurement baseline.
Concern 3 (Planning Structure and Task Definition Maturity): The proposed execution model is excessively broad in scope, spanning across multiple Integrated Product Teams (IPTs) rather than being bounded by logical organizational lines. Due to this oversized design, progress and performance will be difficult to accurately measure or track at the Control Account level. To assess true program status, the Government will
likely need
to track completion and earned value at the lower Work Package/Task level.
Risk: Without defined Work Packages or distinct Control Account boundaries, the Government has no mechanism to measure actual progress or evaluate schedule health. This structure will
likely mask
development bottlenecks, drive severe tracking difficulties, and
render
early Earned Value Management (EVM) and IMS-loading efforts highly inaccurate.

Follow Up 1: P – 2 (Planning Structure and Task Definition Maturity)
Information Gap: The organizational boundaries and tracking mechanisms for cross-IPT Control Accounts are undefined.
Action Needed: Request the contractor deliver a Control Account / IPT Mapping Matrix and a process demonstration showing how they intend to isolate, track, and report progress for work within a single Control Account that is actively being executed by multiple distinct engineering teams.
Follow Up 2: P – 2 (Planning Structure and Task Definition Maturity)
The contractor asserts that detailed plans exist within the P6 scheduling tool, but no evidence has been provided to the Government for review. The Government
requires
read-only access to, or regularly scheduled demonstrations of, the current P6 schedule for this control account. This is necessary to substantiate planning maturity and to enable evidence-based, rather than trust-based, oversight.
