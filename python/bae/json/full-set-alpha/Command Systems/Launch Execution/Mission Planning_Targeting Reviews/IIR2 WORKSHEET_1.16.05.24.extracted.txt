Observations should be supported by objective evidence and reflect the maturity of cross-functional planning, the visibility of interdependencies, and the Government's confidence that the assigned product element can be successfully integrated into the overall program.

4. Concerns and Risks Observed
Document the most significant concerns identified during the assessment and any associated risks that could reduce confidence in the contractor's planning maturity and readiness or adversely affect future planning, integration, or program execution if not addressed.
Concerns should be supported by objective evidence and identify observed weaknesses, gaps, inconsistencies, or uncertainties that warrant additional management attention. Risks should describe the potential consequences if those concerns remain unresolved.
Not every concern creates a program risk, and not every identified risk is currently affecting program execution. The purpose of this section is to identify issues that warrant Government visibility, follow-up, or corrective action as planning continues to mature.
Definitions
Concern: An observed weakness, gap, inconsistency, or uncertainty that may reduce confidence in the contractor's planning maturity and readiness.
Risk: A potential future consequence that could adversely affect planning, cost, schedule, technical performance, integration, PMB implementation, or program execution if a concern is not addressed.
Concern 1 (WBS Index Shift - 1.16.05 vs. 1.16.04):
The contractor has coded this IFC 4.0 database account under WBS 1.16.05.24. The proposed CSDR WBS Dictionary reserves 1.16.5 for IFC 5.0. All IFC 4.0 software tasks must map to WBS 1.16.4 (Full Launch Capability).
Risk 1:
This structural coding mismatch breaks direct program-level cost collection, which will cause compliance and audit failures in CSDR and IPMDAR reporting by blending IFC 4.0 and IFC 5.0 database actuals.
Concern 2 (Database Synchronization Failure Risks):
The Control Node database must dynamically sync data downward to launch silos and upward to wing-wide controllers.
Risk 2:
If the database WBS lines are structurally desynchronized, delays in design updates for one database tier cannot be automatically analyzed for impact on another tier, increasing the risk of database version mismatches during HWIL test campaigns.
5. Open Questions and Follow-Up Items
Document questions that remain unanswered during the assessment and identify the additional evidence, clarification, documentation, or demonstrations needed from the contractor to complete the evaluation or increase confidence in the assessment findings.
Open Questions and Follow-Up Items identify areas where the available evidence was incomplete, unclear, or inconsistent. These items represent information gaps that should be resolved through additional discussions, demonstrations, or artifact reviews before the Government can fully assess the contractor's planning maturity and readiness. They are not, by themselves, concerns, deficiencies, or risks.
Question 1: What is the technical mechanism for target data distribution between the Control Node database (.24) and individual Silo databases (.23)? Is it automated or operator-driven?
Follow-Up 1: Contractor to provide the system/subsystem design description (SSDD) detailing the control node-to-silo data transaction protocols.
Question 2: Will the contractor present a unified correction plan to shift the target catalog control accounts from the 1.16.05 prefix back to 1.16.04 (to map to 1.16.4)?
Follow-Up 2: Contractor to provide a redlined RAM showing the corrected software mappings before baseline locking.

Observation 1 (Middle-Tier Synchronization): The Control Node catalog sits technically between the Wing-Wide Target Catalogs (1.16.05.25) and the Fixed Launch Silo Target Catalogs (1.16.05.23). Tracing these cross-functional database schema dependencies is a major risk when the physical hardware node installations (such as Launch Center Node Integration under WBS 1.4.2.2.3) are mapped separately from the software branch.
Observation 2 (Lack of Automated EVMS Linkage): The physical installation and verification of the control node processing equipment occurs under WBS 1.4.2.2.2.1 (Launch Center Integrated Assemblies). Due to the WBS indexing misalignment, automated systems cannot automatically link schedule delays in hardware to the development of this intermediate database.
