Observations should be supported by objective evidence and reflect the maturity of cross-functional planning, the visibility of interdependencies, and the Government's confidence that the assigned product element can be successfully integrated into the overall program.

4. Concerns and Risks Observed
Document the most significant concerns identified during the assessment and any associated risks that could reduce confidence in the contractor's planning maturity and readiness or adversely affect future planning, integration, or program execution if not addressed.
Concerns should be supported by objective evidence and identify observed weaknesses, gaps, inconsistencies, or uncertainties that warrant additional management attention. Risks should describe the potential consequences if those concerns remain unresolved.
Not every concern creates a program risk, and not every identified risk is currently
affecting program execution. The purpose of this section is to identify issues that warrant Government visibility, follow-up, or corrective action as planning continues to mature.
Definitions
Concern: An observed weakness, gap, inconsistency, or uncertainty that may reduce confidence in the contractor's planning maturity and readiness.
Risk: A potential future consequence that could adversely affect planning, cost, schedule, technical performance, integration, PMB implementation, or program execution if a concern is not addressed.
Concern 1 (Systemic WBS Coding Misalignment / Index Shift):
The contractor has coded this IFC 4.0 control account under WBS 1.16.05.21. However, the proposed CSDR WBS and dictionary designate 1.16.5 for IFC 5.0 (Increment 1 Capability), whereas IFC 4.0 must map to 1.16.4. This is a program-wide index shift (+1) caused by the contractor's insertion of IFC 3.1 as WBS 1.16.04 in their RAM.
Risk 1:
This structural mismatch will corrupt program-wide cost reporting and EVMS metrics, leading to critical compliance failures in IPMDAR and CSDR submissions, and preventing the Government from accurately tracking the true actual costs of the IFC 4.0 and IFC 5.0 releases.
Concern 2 (Cross-Functional Schedule Desynchronization):
Because the software WBS branch is structurally desynchronized from the hardware and site activation WBS branches, matching software release dates with hardware node deployments (e.g., OWSA WCC/LS/LC at FE Warren, under WBS 1.12.2.1.x) will become a manual, error-prone process.
Risk 2:
This desynchronization could lead to schedule delays where hardware sites are activated but the required software (such as IFC 4.0 under this CA) is untraceable or misaligned, stalling integrated testing and deployment.
5. Open Questions and Follow-Up Items

Open Questions and Follow-Up Items identify areas where the available evidence was incomplete, unclear, or inconsistent. These items represent information gaps that should be resolved through additional discussions, demonstrations, or artifact reviews before the Government can fully assess the contractor's planning maturity and readiness. They are not, by themselves, concerns, deficiencies, or risks.
Question 1: What is the technical definition and scope of the "TCG Support Data" referenced in this account, and what specific configuration items (CIs) or computer software configuration items (CSCIs) does it update?
Follow-Up 1: Contractor to provide a detailed walkthrough of the IFC 4.0 feature list (from the IFC Feature Plan/Product Roadmap under SOW 3.16.2.6) and show where "Update TCG Support Data" is mapped.
Question 2: What was the programmatic driver for inserting IFC 3.1 as a level-3 WBS element (1.16.04) in the RAM, and can the contractor demonstrate how they plan to re-align their internal EVMS/RAM coding to match the proposed CSDR WBS?
Follow-Up 2: Contractor to present a corrective action plan to re-align the entire 1.16 Software WBS branch in their RAM to match the proposed CSDR WBS (with IFC 4.0 mapped under 1.16.4).
