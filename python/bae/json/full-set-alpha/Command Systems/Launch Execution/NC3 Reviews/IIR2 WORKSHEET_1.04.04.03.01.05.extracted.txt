Definitions

Risk: A potential future consequence that could adversely affect planning, cost, schedule, technical performance, integration, PMB implementation, or program execution if a concern is not addressed.

These items represent information gaps that should be resolved through additional discussions, demonstrations, or artifact reviews before the Government can fully assess the contractor's planning maturity and readiness. They are not, by themselves, concerns, deficiencies, or risks.
6. Cross-Product/Cross-Function Integration Observations

Cross-Product/Cross-Function Integration Observations should summarize how effectively the contractor has identified, coordinated, and incorporated cross-product/cross-function interfaces, dependencies, and integration activities into its planning architecture. Observations should be supported by objective evidence and reflect the maturity of cross-functional planning, the visibility of interdependencies, and the Government's confidence that the assigned product element can be successfully integrated into the overall program.

Overall Confidence Rating
Rationale
Excessive and Vague Complexity: The current volume of Control Accounts (CAs), combined with poorly defined boundaries, creates a highly complex management matrix. This excessive complexity severely degrades our ability to maintain effective program oversight and puts overall management success at risk.
Inappropriate Risk Transfer: The current CA structure for subcontractors inappropriately shifts internal subcontract management responsibility to the government. This forces the government to absorb substantial programmatic risk without the corresponding contractual authority to manage or mitigate it.
