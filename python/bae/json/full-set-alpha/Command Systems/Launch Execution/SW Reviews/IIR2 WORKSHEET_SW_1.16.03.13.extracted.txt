Assessment Area 1 – Scope and Deliverables Definition
Confidence Rating
(Select One)
Strong:
☐
Moderate:
☐
Low:
☒
Artifacts do not provide enough information to understand the scope of work contained within Software WBS Control Accounts. The transition to Feature-Based Control Accounts has created a critical information gap: current WBS descriptions are insufficiently detailed, and the contractor's reliance on rolling-wave planning means that future Incremental Features (Work Packages) are not defined upfront to establish a
baseline. While near-term scope is detailed inside Jira, program leadership must have high-level scope clarity directly within the baseline planning artifacts rather than being forced to search through execution-level software tools.
While a product-oriented, feature-based structure is conceptually appropriate for agile software, the decomposition stops at too high a level for the baseline.
To establish confidence in the PMB, the contractor must provide artifacts containing detailed descriptions of the specific software capabilities captured within each System Feature and Control Account. This level of understanding requires either:
1. A predefined list of associated Incremental Features / Work Packages, or
2. Significantly enhanced, highly detailed descriptions of the Control Accounts within the CWBS Dictionary.
The contractor’s proposed rolling-wave planning approach dictates that they “will decompose the Incremental Features from the Planning Packages prior to the start of the next PI and after Incremental Feature lockdown agreement across Command Systems.” While rolling-wave planning is standard practice in Agile development, performing decomposition of Incremental Features until right before execution prevents the Government from establishing an upfront baseline. The Government cannot verify, measure, or have confidence in the scope of a Control Account when the underlying Work Packages are entirely undefined.
For the current and immediately upcoming Program Increments (PIs), the contractor does provide detailed decomposition; however, this data exists solely within their internal Jira instance.
While Jira is the correct tool for day-to-day engineering execution, it is not a substitute for formal program management artifacts. Government Program Managers and Analysts should not be required to dig through active developer backlogs and software management tools to piece together a basic understanding of a Control Account’s contractual scope. This information must be maintained and easily accessible within the contract baseline documentation.
Assessment Area 2 – Schedule Planning and IMS-Loading Readiness
Confidence Rating
(Select One)
Strong:
☐
Moderate:
☐
Low:
☒
While the contractor has established a conceptually sound planning hierarchy and logical workflows, this framework remains largely unpopulated. The foundational ambiguity regarding Control Account scope (identified in Assessment Area 1) fundamentally prevents the contractor from translating their theoretical planning structure into an executable, resource-loaded Integrated Master Schedule (IMS).
The contractor has successfully demonstrated a mature theoretical approach to schedule planning. The designated workflow effectively maps how software capabilities will transition through the engineering lifecycle, moving systematically from Feature Definition into Systems Engineering, through Software Development, and culminating in Integration & Test. Furthermore, the methodology for breaking these phases down into Incremental Features and Work Packages within their Jira and QBD constructs is logical and repeatable.
Despite possessing a viable planning architecture, the contractor lacks the foundational data required to execute it. Schedule development cannot occur in a vacuum; it is fundamentally dependent on a clear understanding of the scope. Because the contractor cannot currently articulate the specific scope boundaries of each Control Account, they are subsequently unable to populate their planning hierarchy with actual tasks, durations, or resource requirements.
In its current state, the contractor has built a robust schedule planning engine but has not provided the fuel (scope and task data) required to run it. Until the underlying scope is clearly defined and the associated data is entered into the planning constructs, the contractor is not ready for credible IMS loading or Performance Measurement Baseline (PMB) implementation.

These items represent information gaps that should be resolved through additional discussions, demonstrations, or artifact reviews before the Government can fully assess the contractor's planning maturity and readiness. They are not, by themselves, concerns, deficiencies, or risks.
Follow-up 1: S – 1 Information Gap: The Government lacks visibility into the total planned software scope prior to PI execution. Action Needed: Request the contractor provide a preliminary software roadmap, capability matrix, or enhanced CWBS Dictionary that explicitly maps specific software functionalities to the proposed Feature-Based Control Accounts to establish a baseline understanding of scope prior to detailed PI decomposition.
Follow-up 2: I – 1 Unresolved Assumption: It is unclear how agile software delivery will synchronize with hardware schedules. Action Needed: Require the contractor to present a HW/SW Integration Strategy. The contractor must demonstrate exactly how agile software milestones will map to, constrain, or enable traditional hardware integration events.
Follow-up 3: P – 2 (Planning Structure and Task Definition Maturity)
Information Gap: The organizational boundaries and tracking mechanisms for cross-IPT Control Accounts are undefined.
Action Needed: Request the contractor deliver a Control Account / IPT Mapping Matrix and a process demonstration showing how they intend to isolate, track, and report progress for work within a single Control Account that is actively being executed by multiple distinct engineering teams.

Observation 1: Hardware/Software Integration & PI Alignment
There is a critical scheduling and data dependency between the Agile software development cadence and traditional hardware integration milestones. Software releases are planned via Program Increments (PIs), but Hardware nodes require specific software baselines at fixed dates to conduct physical integration and checkout. Not clear where the mapped alignment between the exists.
Observation 2: Shared Test Environments (System Integration Lab - SIL)
The software development teams are dependent on physical or virtual test environments (e.g., SILs or digital twins) provided by the Hardware and Infrastructure teams. Software cannot complete its defined Integration & Test phase without access to these shared resources. Coordination is required to ensure that the hardware team prioritizes the build-out and availability of the SIL environments to match the software team's PI testing schedule.
