Integrated Implementation Review (IIR)
Control Account 1.10.01.04.07
LS-SPT Prototype Support Equipment Development – Design & Procurement
Executive Summary
This Control Account manages the design, development, procurement, fabrication, and delivery of prototype support equipment required for the LS-SPT program. The WBS indicates a well-planned prototype development effort emphasizing early hardware availability, design validation, supplier coordination, and support for integration and test activities. The control account demonstrates a logical progression from engineering outputs to prototype hardware delivery.Overall Health: GREENOverall Confidence: HIGH (90%)
Control Account Purpose
Develop prototype support equipment designs.
Procure prototype hardware and long-lead materials.
Coordinate supplier fabrication activities.
Support prototype manufacturing and assembly.
Deliver prototype hardware for integration and testing.
Maintain configuration control throughout prototype development.
Scope Assessment
Prototype development activities are logically decomposed.
Design and procurement activities are clearly separated.
Work packages are executable and measurable.
Planning packages support future prototype efforts.
Scope supports engineering, manufacturing, and test organizations.
Plan for the capture of logistics data (provisioning, maintenance procedures, technical data) during prototype development.
Design & Prototype Development
Prototype designs support early verification of engineering concepts.
Design maturity should be confirmed prior to procurement.
Prototype hardware provides valuable technical risk reduction.
Engineering changes should be tightly controlled.
Included logistics and supportability criteria in design reviews to influence prototype design
Procurement & Supplier Assessment
Supplier engagement supports prototype schedules.
Long-lead material planning is appropriate.
Prototype procurement should remain configuration controlled.
Supplier performance should be monitored throughout fabrication.
Supplier contracts should require deliverables (provisioning data, tech data, spares) for prototype hardware.
Manufacturing Readiness
Prototype fabrication activities support production learning.
Manufacturing processes should be documented for future production.
Tooling and specialized equipment availability should be verified.
Prototype build lessons learned should be captured.
Integration & Test Support
Prototype hardware supports integration planning.
Early hardware availability reduces downstream program risk.
Engineering & logistic participation during testing is recommended.
Test feedback should be incorporated into design updates.
Risk Assessment
Engineering changes remain the primary technical risk.
Supplier delays may impact prototype schedules.
Prototype failures should be captured as design learning opportunities.
Overall execution risk assessed as LOW–MODERATE.
Monitor supply chain risk in the risk register.
EVMS / IBR Assessment
Clearly defined prototype scope.
Strong linkage between engineering and procurement.
Executable work package structure.
Planning supports measurable earned value.
No significant structural deficiencies identified.
Report readiness metrics as part of EVMS/IBR reviews.
Recommended Actions
Maintain engineering baseline stability before procurement.
Conduct recurring supplier readiness reviews.
Capture prototype lessons learned for production planning.
Monitor long-lead material procurement closely.
Maintain configuration management discipline.
Logistics SME participation in all technical and integration reviews.
Overall Reviewer Conclusion
This Control Account reflects a mature prototype development strategy supporting a major Department of Defense acquisition program. The WBS demonstrates appropriate planning for prototype design, procurement, fabrication, and integration. Prototype execution is expected to reduce technical risk prior to production while providing valuable manufacturing and integration experience. Overall assessment: IIR Ready with HIGH confidence.
Continued logistics integration and proactive sustainment planning will ensure prototypes are not only technically sound, but also fully supportable and ready for seamless transition to production. This approach embeds sustainment requirements from the outset, maximizing readiness and minimizing lifecycle risk.
