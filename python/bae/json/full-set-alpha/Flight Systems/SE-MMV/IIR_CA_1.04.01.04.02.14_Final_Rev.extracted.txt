Integrated Implementation Review (IIR)
Control Account 1.04.01.04.02.14
LS-SPT MMIII C&L Support Equipment Systems Engineering
Executive Summary
This Control Account manages the systems engineering activities supporting the MMIII C&L Support Equipment effort. The WBS demonstrates a mature systems engineering approach covering requirements, architecture, interfaces, technical baseline management, verification planning, and integration support. The technical planning is logically sequenced and provides a strong foundation for downstream design, procurement, and integration.Overall Health: GREENOverall Confidence: HIGH (91%).
Control Account Purpose
Develop and maintain technical requirements.
Manage system architecture and technical baselines.
Perform interface definition and control.
Support verification and validation planning.
Provide engineering support for design, procurement, and integration.
Maintain configuration management throughout the engineering lifecycle.
Scope Assessment
Requirements decomposition is complete and logically organized.
Engineering activities are separated from hardware realization.
Work packages are executable and measurable.
Planning packages are appropriately reserved for future work.
Scope supports the complete systems engineering lifecycle.
Sustainment requirements are included in the systems engineering scope and mapped to WBS elements
Technical Baseline Assessment
Requirements traceability supports technical maturity.
Architecture products support downstream implementation.
Interfaces are identified early to reduce integration risk.
Configuration control supports baseline stability.
Continue managing engineering changes through formal review boards.
Requirements & Architecture
Requirements appear aligned with program objectives.
Architecture development supports integration planning.
Technical documentation provides a basis for verification.
Maintain bidirectional traceability throughout design maturation.
Requirements are traced from SOW for design and verification.
Verification & Validation
Verification planning begins early in the lifecycle.
Requirements verification strategy appears established.
Test planning supports downstream integration activities.
Continue updating verification matrices as requirements evolve.
Integration Assessment
Systems engineering supports successful hardware integration.
Interface management reduces integration risk.
Engineering & logistics participation throughout integration is recommended.
Coordinate closely with Design & Procurement control accounts.
Risk Assessment
Requirement changes remain the highest technical risk.
Interface modifications may impact downstream integration.
Configuration drift should be minimized through strict change control.
Overall technical risk assessed as LOW–MODERATE.
Manages supply chain risk.
EVMS / IBR Assessment
Strong technical decomposition.
Clearly defined engineering scope.
Logical work package organized.
Good alignment with systems engineering best practices.
No significant structural deficiencies identified.
Recommended Actions
Maintain rigorous requirements traceability.
Continue recurring interface control working groups.
Monitor planning package conversion before execution.
Perform regular technical baseline reviews.
Maintain close coordination with procurement and integration organizations.
Logistics participates in all design reviews to influence decisions for maintainability, accessibility, and lifecycle support.
Overall Reviewer Conclusion
This Control Account exhibits the characteristics of a mature Systems Engineering organization supporting a complex Department of Defense acquisition program. The engineering scope is comprehensive, technically sound, and appropriately structured to support downstream design, procurement, integration, and verification. Remaining risks are typical of systems engineering efforts and are manageable through disciplined configuration management, technical reviews, and stakeholder coordination. Overall assessment: IIR Ready with HIGH confidence. From a logistics and O&S perspective, the account is fundamentally sound, but sustained, shoulder-to-shoulder engagement between logistics and engineering is essential for designing a supportable, maintainable, and affordable system. Logistics must be a proactive partner in every systems engineering activity to ensure long-term O&S success.
